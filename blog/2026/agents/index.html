Thoughts on agentic coding

A quest to regain joy in the age of agentic coding

Contents

Mandarin
English

Mandarin

写下这篇博客的这一刻，我感到非常的陌生。曾几何时，十来岁的我在一格一格的作文纸上，用圆珠笔在三十分钟内写下一篇八百字的作文，几十次考试里写了一次又一次。可是现在，我已经记不得多久之前我完整的用自己的话写下一篇文章。没有ai，没有复制粘贴，不是改什么，而是从头开始写。

我从不是一个文采飞扬的人，但是，我也曾在手搓文字和代码中获取过极大的快乐。那是一种在心流里的感觉， 是心理学中的optimal experience (quote Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi)。

我尤其热爱手写代码。曾经，我用一周的时间写出过一个软件包，白天去研究所，写代码，晚上回家，休整过后，继续写到半夜。英国冬天十分阴冷，写了几天之后我感觉头顶发凉，发际线减退，手脚很冰，但是我的精神无比的充足。一个一个方程，各自做着他们应该做的事，一句一句注释，穿起完整的思路。一个软件包，做一件特定的，没有人做出来过的事。这段时光给我带来了一篇论文，更重要的是，它带来了永远印在我脑海里的epiphany。从此以后，我知道我自己可以做成一件事，推动科学知识的边界一点点，只靠我自己。

后来呢，在我博士研究的后半段，coding agent，它来了。你跟它说自然语言，它能在一分钟之内产出一堆的方程。一开始，它很混乱。明显是从各种不同风格条件的开源代码里拼拼凑凑，拼出来勉强能用但是品味很差的代码。逐渐的，它变得越来越清晰和强大。大家发明了skills, memory, mcp server，用人类喜欢的条条框框逐渐的去规训agent的输出。它好像变得越来越认真，从一个随便用着玩玩的聊天框，进化成了好像本来就应该在研究管线里的关键劳动力。

开始工作以后，2026年，我和我身边的所有人都在用claude code写代码。刚开始我和我的同事们都是半激动半抗拒的，我相信大家喜欢生物信息这一行，喜欢软件工程，都是因为有过类似的美好经历。现在呢，可以说是为了效率，或者是大势所趋，一个月几十美刀就可以让一个coder放弃手搓代码，改成不停的点accept和偶尔抱怨一下agent的奇葩输出。甚至呢，最近有很多的订阅式coding assistant 合了各种不同公司的llm，自己提升自己，一个晚上就可以写出一个复杂的app，已经代替掉一部分的决策了。

我不知道有多少人和我一样，感觉好像被剥夺了某种职业带来的成就感。我知道我肯定不是一个人。是的，我依然有做决策的责任，多年研究带来的经验和品位没有改变，我们也暂时没有真正的得到科学和医学的大进步。但是对于一个人，科学家，工程师，尤其是需要在个人生活和职业道路之间做出选择的我，我很难过的得出结论，我的工作没有之前那么令我快乐了。

再过一年，我的职业会变成什么样子呢。好玩的是，我几乎是出于本能的不断提升自己使用agents的能力。因为我的职业素养告诉我，必须要尽快接受和熟练的使用这一类工具。我热衷于在工作内外用agents做程序，做网页，做视频。前几个月的淡淡不适，在现在已经几乎变成了冷静的麻木。

因此，我想在这里记录下我的经历。数据中心对自然的破坏，科技股票和金属的大起大落，以及我们都知道这些超高估值的科技公司都在疯狂负债。这些问题都不容小觑。可以感激的是，我现在写下这些文字已经觉得顺利多了。

English

As I write this blog post, I feel strangely out of practice. Once upon a time, as a teenager, I would sit with a ballpoint pen over a sheet of squared marked paper and write an eight-hundred-character Mandarin essay in thirty minutes. I did it in exam after exam, dozens of times. Now I cannot remember when I last wrote a whole piece in my own words. No AI, no copy and paste, no editing something that already existed. Just writing from scratch.

I have never been a gifted writer, but I have found enormous joy in crafting words and code by hand. It is the feeling of being in flow, what psychology calls the optimal experience (quote Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi).

Writing code by hand is what I loved most. Once during my PhD, I wrote an entire software package in a week. I went to the institute during the day and wrote code. In the evening I went home, rested, and then kept writing until the middle of the night. The English winter were damp and cold. After a few days of this my scalp felt chilly, my hairline seemed to be retreating, and my hands and feet were freezing, but my mind was more alive than ever. One function after another, each doing exactly what it was supposed to do. One comment after another, threading a complete line of thought. One package, doing one specific thing that nobody had done before. That work gave me a paper. More importantly, it gave me an epiphany that is imprinted on me forever. From then on, I knew that I could make something happen and push the boundary of scientific knowledge just a little further, relying on myself alone.

And then, in the second half of my PhD, coding agents arrived. You speak to it in natural language and it produces a bunch of functions within a minute. At first it was a mess. It was clearly stitched together from open-source code of every style and convention, and the result barely worked and had terrible taste. Gradually it became clearer and more powerful. People invented skills, memory, and MCP servers, and used the rules and structures humans like to gradually discipline the agent’s output. The whole situation started to become more serious, evolving from a chat bot you played with for fun into a key worker that looked like it had always belonged in the research pipeline.

After I started working, in 2026, we were all writing code with Claude. At the beginning my colleagues and I were half excited and half resistant. I believe we all came to bioinformatics and software engineering because we had experienced something like that joy. Now, whether for the sake of efficiency or simply because this is where things are going, a few dozen dollars a month is enough to make a coder give up writing code by hand and switch to pressing accept over and over, with the occasional complaint about the agent’s bizarre output. Recently, subscription coding assistants have even started combining LLMs from different companies, and improving themselves by checking on eachother. They can build a complex app in a single night, and they have already taken over part of the decision making.

I am not certain how others feel, as I do, that a certain kind of professional fulfilment has been taken away from them. I know I am not the only one. Yes, I still carry the responsibility for the decisions. The experience and taste built up over years of research have not changed. And we have not yet seen a true leap in science or medicine. But as a person, a scientist, an engineer, and especially as someone who has to choose between personal life and career path, I have sadly come to the conclusion that my work makes me less happy than it used to.

What will my profession look like in a year? The funny thing is that, almost by instinct, I keep sharpening my ability to use agents. My professional sense tells me I must accept these tools and become fluent with them as fast as possible. I enthusiastically use agents, inside and outside work, to build programs, web pages, and videos. The faint unease of a few months ago has by now turned into a calm numbness.

So I want to record my experience here. The damage data centres do to nature, the wild swings in tech stocks and metals, and the fact that we all know these sky-high-valuation tech companies are piling on debt at a frantic pace. None of these problems should be taken lightly. What I can be grateful for is that, by the time I finish writing these words, the writing already feels much smoother.
